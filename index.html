001 / The Movement

A theology assembled in public, by nobody in particular

The Pleiadian New Age movement has no founder, no institution and no settled text. What it has is a literature — channelled, largely self-published, mostly written after 1988 — and an oral tradition that travels faster and further than the literature does.

The result is a belief system held by a very large number of people who agree on the broad shape and almost nothing else. Ask ten adherents what a soul contract is and you will get ten answers, most of them descended from Gnostic texts written eighteen centuries before anyone connected the Pleiades to human destiny. Ask where the Galactic Federation came from and the trail runs back through a small number of named channels, a disputed Sumerian translation, and a handful of paperbacks from the 1970s.

This outline follows every trail to its end. Where a teaching has a documented origin, that origin is named, with the author and the year. Where a teaching has been reassigned from an older tradition, the older tradition is identified. Where a claim can be checked against the historical, archaeological or scientific record, it is checked, and the result is reported plainly.

It is not a devotional text and it does not pretend to be one. It is a map — the first one drawn of this territory in full.

003 / Method

How it was assembled

Every teaching in this outline was traced to the earliest source that states it. Channelled material is cited to the channel and the year. Borrowed material is cited to the tradition it came from. Field accounts are reproduced as the participants gave them, with the claim and the checkable record printed side by side and no attempt made to reconcile them on the reader's behalf. Where a claim is unfalsifiable, it is marked unfalsifiable rather than argued with. Where a claim is checkable and fails, that is stated once and the document moves on. This is the apparatus of a reference work applied to a body of belief that has never had one.

004 / Field Records · § D

Three accounts, recorded as given

The movement's practice is not only textual. Adherents travel to sites they consider significant and perform workings there. Three such expeditions are documented in § D, from the accounts of named participants credited in the document.

Each is reproduced without editorial smoothing. Where a participant's account touches something on the public record — a seismic event, a glaciological survey, a named geological formation — that record is printed alongside, and the reader is left to hold both.

01 / Iceland & Ireland

The volcanic working

A two-person party carrying tuning forks, an approach flown over the Greenland ice, and a working performed at a volcanic site. The account includes an eruption in the days following, an incident involving one participant and the vegetation at the site, and the decision to complete the circuit in Ireland rather than fly home. The sequence of dates is set against the published seismic record.

02 / Greenland

The ice-free ground

Aerial photographs taken in transit, showing terrain the participants read as installations, alongside ground bare of ice at latitudes where ice is expected. The section reproduces the images, states what is visible in them, states separately what is inferred from them, and sets that inference beside the published glaciological explanation for the anomaly.

Aerial plates · Claim and counter-explanation printed together
03 / The Utah Desert

Behind Moab

A party of twelve in the desert behind Moab. Petrified formations photographed and read as figurative. A photograph taken at a plateau. A working performed at a crystal site. And a free-standing sandstone structure the party compared to Petra. Location, geology and the formation's documented name are all given, so a reader can go and stand where they stood.

007 / Questions

Frequently asked

Is this written by a believer?

No. It is written from the outside in a neutral register. Beliefs are reported accurately and without mockery, then followed by a plain note on what the historical and scientific record actually supports. If you are looking for confirmation, this is the wrong document.

Will it tell me whether I am a starseed?

No. It makes no claim about you, offers no reading, diagnosis or identification, and predicts nothing. It describes what the movement teaches about starseeds and traces where that teaching came from.

Is it a religious text?

No. It is a reference document about a body of belief. It has no liturgy, no instruction and no claim to authority of its own. It asks you to accept nothing.

Why nineteen pages instead of a book?

Because it is an outline, and the structure is the point. Padded to three hundred pages the shape would disappear. You can read it in one sitting and use it as a reference for years afterward.
